    In my first post-doc, I have been working on the 3D'omics project
    aiming at developing a 3D-based framework to disentangle microbe-microbe and animal-microbe interactions.
    Over this time, I focused on microbial interactions using metabolic modeling based approaches.

    In a recent study of ours, we were able to show that 
    predicted growth rates and their ratios 
    (i.e., the predicted interaction strength between two taxa), 
    do not correlate with~\textit{in vitro} data~\cite{joseph2024predicting}; 
    however, curated GEMs had more reliable results.
    To address this limitation, we have just released the first beta-version of a microbial growth data repository, 
    called \href{https://mgrowthdb.gbiomed.kuleuven.be/}{{$\mu$}growthDB}
    (https://mgrowthdb.gbiomed.kuleuven.be/).
    The broader the endorsement by the microbiology community, 
    the more effectively it will enable us to constrain models with higher accuracy.

    My main focus though was~\textit{microbetag}~(https://microbetag.readthedocs.io/) 
    a software ecosystem for annotating co-occurrence microbial networks.
    microbetag annotates taxa (nodes) with phenotypic traits, based on genomic and literature information,
    and their predicted associations (edges) with potential metabolic interactions, based on the \textit{seed} concept. 
    We consider seeds the compounds that are present and can be used from a reaction in a GEM, but no internal reaction 
    is producing them, thus the species would have to get them from the environment to enable the corresponding reaction(s).
    Combining such annotations with network clustering algorithms and enrichment analysis, we are now able to 
    produce specific hypotheses on what may drive a system.
    Only last week, \textit{microbetag} was accepted in principle for publication in 
    \textit{Genome Biology}~\cite{zafeiropoulos2024microbetag}. 


    However, it is my belief, that what depicts my understanding on how microbial communities are shaped and evolved, 
    but also my personal scientific motivation, is my proposal for the
    European Molecular Biology Organization (EMBO) postodoctoral fellowship program,
    which made it to the interviews stage. 
    My main hypothesis was that the auxotrophies a species develops are
    related to its resource hierarchy—that is, its nutrient preferences. 
    Moreover, since species in natural communities never exist in isolation, 
    metabolic interactions with other community members would also influence auxotrophies, 
    while resource hierarchies simultaneously shape community structure in a feedback loop.
    Working further this framework, I believe will lead to great insight on the mechanisms communities are shaped.

    In our perspective paper on metabolic modeling on human gut microbiome~\cite{garza2023metabolic}
    we highlight how one can a set of different modeling approaches using a toy model of three common 
    gut species with different metabolic capabilities: a carbohydrate fermenter, an acetogen, 
    and a butyrate producer.
    We display how one can use kinetic models, 
    inability to handle flexible metabolic responses means that in many cases it does not meet
    Einstein's famous 'as simple as possible but not simpler' criterium,
    and thus, we argue on the added value metabolic modeling can support. 

    In the following sections I provide more details on my previous projects, see for example our evaluation study 
    for FBA-based interaction strength predictions~\cite{joseph2024predicting}.
    Yet here, I would like to point out my
    ongoing project on \textit{Salmonella infantis} and a set of probiotics known to decrease its abundance.
    To decipher how this is happening, whether all probiotics are required for the decrease to occur  
    and whether the interactions among any of these strains are metabolically-driven,
    I am working with a set of \textit{in vitro} data from mono- and co-culture communities, 
    and the GEMs of the corresponding taxa. 

    A manuscript for this project is in preparation.
